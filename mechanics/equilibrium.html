Equilibrium

A body stays at rest only if the forces on it cancel and their moments about any point cancel too:

Worked example

Uses the values in Fig. 1.2-1. Change them and the working updates.

Given
  • Whanging weight500 N
  • θ1left cable, from horizontal40°
  • θ2right cable, from horizontal60°
Find

The tension in each cable.

Solution
ΣFx = 0: T1 cos θ1 = T2 cos θ2 ΣFy = 0: T1 sin θ1 + T2 sin θ2 = W T1 = W cos θ2 / sin(θ1 + θ2) = 500 N × cos 60° / sin(40° + 60°) = 254 N T2 = W cos θ1 / sin(θ1 + θ2) = 500 N × cos 40° / sin(40° + 60°) = 389 N
Answer
T₁ = 254 N, T₂ = 389 N

Check: T₁ sin θ₁ + T₂ sin θ₂ = 500 N, the weight.

Assumptions
  • The cables are weightless, do not stretch and can only pull.
  • All three forces meet at the knot, so their moments about it are zero and ΣM = 0 holds automatically. ΣF = 0 does the work.
  • The anchors are rigid.

Derivation

Draw the free body of the knot: T1 pulls up and to the left at θ1, T2 up and to the right at θ2, and the weight W pulls straight down.

  1. Horizontal: T1 cos θ1 = T2 cos θ2.
  2. Vertical: T1 sin θ1 + T2 sin θ2 = W.
  3. Put T2 = T1 cos θ1 / cos θ2 into the vertical equation and multiply by cos θ2: T1(sin θ1 cos θ2 + cos θ1 sin θ2) = W cos θ2.
  4. The bracket is sin(θ1 + θ2), so T1 = W cos θ2 / sin(θ1 + θ2).

Swapping the subscripts gives T2. As both cables flatten, the sine in the denominator shrinks and the tensions grow without limit, which is why a loaded line can never be pulled perfectly straight.
