Mass–spring–damper

Pull a mass on a spring and let go: it bobs at a frequency set only by its mass and the spring's stiffness. Add a damper and the motion dies away; how fast, and whether it overshoots, depends on how the damping compares with a critical value.

Find

Solution

One cycle takes 0.628 s, and the mass passes the rest line at 0.4 m/s.

Worked example: damping

Uses the values in Fig. 5.1-2. Change them and the working updates.

Given
  • mmass2 kg
  • kspring stiffness200 N/m
  • cdamping coefficient10 N·s/m
Find

The critical damping, the damping ratio, and whether the system is under-, critically or overdamped.

Solution
cc = 2√(km) = 2√(200 N/m × 2 kg) = 40 N·s/m ζ = c/cc = 10 / 40 = 0.25 ωn = √(k/m) = √(200 N/m / 2 kg) = 10 rad/s ωd = ωn√(1 − ζ²) = 10 rad/s × √(1 − 0.25²) = 9.68 rad/s
Answer
ζ = 0.25

ζ is below 1, so it is underdamped and oscillates as it decays.

Assumptions
  • The spring is linear (force kx) and massless, and the damper is viscous (force cẋ).
  • The mass moves only up and down. Displacement is measured from where it hangs at rest, so gravity drops out.
  • Part 1 has no damping, so the oscillation never dies away. Part 2 releases the mass from rest at x0.

Derivation
  1. Newton's second law for the mass: mẍ + cẋ + kx = 0.
  2. With no damping, x = A cos ωnt solves it when ωn² = k/m.
  3. With damping, try x = ert: mr² + cr + k = 0, so r = (−c ± √(c² − 4km)) / 2m.
  4. The roots change character where c² = 4km, at the critical damping cc = 2√(km). Writing ζ = c/cc gives r = −ζωn ± ωn√(ζ² − 1).
  5. For ζ < 1 the roots are complex: a decaying oscillation at ωd = ωn√(1 − ζ²). At ζ = 1 they coincide, and for ζ > 1 both are real and negative: decay with no oscillation.

Critical damping returns the mass to rest fastest without overshooting. Car suspension sits well below it, around ζ ≈ 0.2–0.4, trading a little overshoot for comfort; a door closer runs near or above it.
