Jacking

In a corner, load moves from the inner wheels to the outer ones. The roll centre height decides how: the share above it rolls the body onto the springs, and the share below it goes straight through the suspension links, jacking the body up.

Worked example

Uses the values in Fig. 3.1-1. Change them and the working updates.

Given
  • aylateral acceleration1 g
  • hCG height400 mm
  • ttrack width1,600 mm
  • hrcroll centre height90 mm
Find

How the load transfer splits between jacking and roll, and how much of the car's weight moves across.

Solution
ΔWJ/ΔW = hrc/h = 90 mm / 400 mm = 22.5% ΔWR/ΔW = 1 − hrc/h = 1 − 22.5% = 77.5% ΔW/mg = (ay/g)·h/t = 1 × 400 mm / 1,600 mm = 25%
Answer
Jacking = 22.5%

Roll takes the other 77.5%. 25% of the car's weight moves to the outer wheels.

Assumptions
  • Steady cornering on a level road. The whole car is treated as one axle, with one roll centre and one track.
  • The roll centre lies on the centreline and stays put. In practice it moves as the body rolls and the suspension compresses.
  • Roll angle is small, so the CG's sideways shift as the body rolls is ignored.
  • "Jacking share" is the load that goes through the links. The vertical jacking force that comes with it is under Related forms.

Derivation
  1. Cornering puts an inertia force may at the CG, height h. Moments about the outer contact patch give the total transfer, ΔW = mayh/t.
  2. Move the force down to the roll centre. That leaves the same force at height hrc, plus a roll moment may(h − hrc).
  3. A sideways force at the roll centre causes no roll, so it passes straight through the links: ΔWJ = mayhrc/t.
  4. The roll moment rolls the body onto the springs and anti-roll bars: ΔWR = may(h − hrc)/t.
  5. Dividing each by ΔW: the jacking share is hrc/h and the roll share 1 − hrc/h.

The total transfer does not depend on the roll centre; only the split does. A low roll centre means a long roll arm and more body roll. A high one means less roll but more jacking. Below ground, the jacking share turns negative: the links pull the body down and the springs carry more than the whole transfer.
