Weight transfer under braking

When a car brakes, the inertia of its mass acts at the centre of gravity and pitches load off the rear axle onto the front. The load transferred is:

Worked example

Uses the values in Fig. 2.1-1. Change them and the working updates.

Given
  • mvehicle mass1,500 kg
  • axdeceleration0.8 g
  • hCG height550 mm
  • Lwheelbase2,700 mm
  • aCG behind front axle1,215 mm
Find

The load transferred to the front axle ΔW, and the axle loads while braking.

Solution
ax = 0.8 × 9.81 m/s² = 7.85 m/s² ΔW = maxh/L = 1,500 kg × 7.85 m/s² × 0.55 m / 2.7 m = 2.4 kN Wfront = mg(L − a)/L + ΔW = 14.7 kN × 1.485 m / 2.7 m + 2.4 kN = 10.5 kN Wrear = mga/L − ΔW = 14.7 kN × 1.215 m / 2.7 m − 2.4 kN = 4.22 kN
Answer
ΔW = 2.4 kN

The front axle now carries 71.3% of the weight, up from 55%.

Assumptions
  • The car is a rigid body. Suspension pitch, which moves the centre of gravity slightly, is ignored.
  • The road is level and aerodynamic forces are ignored.
  • h is the centre of gravity's height above the ground.
  • Total weight is unchanged: load moves between the axles. If the rear load reaches zero, the rear wheels lift.

Derivation
  1. At rest, moments about the rear tyre contact patch give WfrontL = mgb.
  2. Braking adds an inertia force max, acting forwards at the centre of gravity, height h. The braking forces act at ground level, so they have no moment about the contact patch.
  3. So WfrontL = mgb + maxh, and the front axle gains ΔW = maxh/L.
  4. Vertical balance: the rear axle loses the same amount.

Transfer grows with CG height and shrinks with wheelbase. That is why long, low cars pitch less under braking.
