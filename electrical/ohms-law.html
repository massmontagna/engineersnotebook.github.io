Ohm's law

In a resistor at constant temperature, the current is proportional to the voltage across it. The constant is the resistance:

V = IR

Worked example

Uses the values in Fig. 1.1-1. Change them and the working updates.

Given
  • Vvoltage across the resistor12 V
  • Rresistance100 Ω
Find

The current I.

Solution
I = V/R = 12 V / 100 Ω = 0.12 A
Answer
I = 120 mA

The resistor dissipates P = VI = 1.44 W as heat.

Assumptions
  • The resistor is ohmic: its resistance stays constant. That holds for resistors and metal wires at steady temperature, but not for diodes, or lamps as they warm up.
  • The source and connecting wires are ideal, with no resistance of their own.
  • Direct current, or AC through a pure resistance.

Derivation
  1. In a metal, free electrons drift at a speed proportional to the electric field, so the current density is J = σeE, where σe is the conductivity.
  2. Along a uniform wire, E = V/L and J = I/A, so I/A = σeV/L.
  3. Rearrange: V = I · L/(σeA) = IR, with R = ρL/A and ρ = 1/σe.

Related cases

  • Series

    R = R1 + R2

  • Parallel

    1R = 1R1 + 1R2

  • Voltage divider

    Vout = V R2R1 + R2

  • Resistance of a wire

    R = ρLA
