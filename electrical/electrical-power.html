Electrical power

Power is the rate at which a circuit converts electrical energy into heat, light or work. It is voltage times current:

P = VI

Worked example

Uses the values in Fig. 1.2-1. Change them and the working updates.

Given
  • Vsupply voltage230 V
  • Icurrent drawn5 A
  • ttime switched on1 h
Find

The power P, and the energy used in time t.

Solution
P = VI = 230 V × 5 A = 1,150 W E = Pt = 1.15 kW × 1 h = 1.15 kWh
Answer
P = 1.15 kW

The equivalent resistance is V/I = 46 Ω, so P = I²R gives the same 1.15 kW.

Assumptions
  • Direct current, or AC with RMS values and a resistive load (power factor 1).
  • For loads with a power factor below 1, such as motors, multiply by cos φ to get the real power.
  • Voltage and current stay steady for the whole time t.

Derivation
  1. Voltage is energy per unit charge: V = E/q.
  2. Current is charge per unit time: I = q/t.
  3. Multiply them: VI = E/t, which is power.
  4. With Ohm's law, V = IR, this becomes P = I²R = V²/R.

One kilowatt-hour is 1 kW for one hour, or 3.6 MJ.

Related forms

  • Current and resistance

    P = I²R

  • Voltage and resistance

    P = V²R

  • Three-phase supply

    P = √3 VLIL cos φ
