First law

Energy is conserved. Heat Q added to a closed system either raises its internal energy U or leaves as work W done by the system:

ΔU = Q − W

Worked example

Uses the values in Fig. 1.2-1. Change them and the working updates.

Given
  • Qheat added5 kJ
  • namount of gas1 mol
  • cvdiatomic gas (air, N₂, O₂)2.5R
  • processconstant pressure
Find

The change in internal energy ΔU.

Solution
c = cₚ = cᵥ + R = 29.1 J/(mol·K) ΔT = Q/nc = 5,000 J / (1 mol × 29.1 J/(mol·K)) = 172 K W = nRΔT = 1 mol × 8.314 J/(mol·K) × 172 K = 1.43 kJ ΔU = Q − W = 5 kJ − 1.43 kJ = 3.57 kJ
Answer
ΔU = 3.57 kJ

71.4% of the heat went into internal energy, raising the temperature by 172 K.

Assumptions
  • The system is closed: no mass crosses its boundary.
  • Heat into the system and work done by it are positive. Some texts count work done on the system instead and write ΔU = Q + W.
  • The gas is ideal with constant heat capacities, so U depends on temperature alone.
  • The process is slow enough that the gas is always close to equilibrium.
  • Changes in the system's bulk kinetic and potential energy are ignored.

Derivation
  1. Energy is conserved, so heat in either stays in the gas or leaves as work: Q = ΔU + W.
  2. For an ideal gas, U depends only on temperature: ΔU = ncvΔT.
  3. At constant pressure the gas pushes its boundary out: W = p ΔV = nR ΔT.
  4. So Q = n(cv + R)ΔT = ncpΔT, which gives ΔT from Q.

At constant volume no work is done, so all of the heat raises U: Q = ncvΔT.

Related cases

  • Isothermal, ideal gas

    ΔU = 0Q = W = nRT ln(V2V1)

  • Adiabatic, no heat

    Q = 0ΔU = −W

  • Complete cycle

    ΔU = 0Qnet = Wnet
